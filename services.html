Every Water Heater Service We Offer in Tempe

Every water heater has a working life, and in the Valley that life runs shorter than the national average. Hard water at 15 to 25 grains per gallon lays sediment inside tanks, wears down anode rods, and pushes tank lifespan to 6 to 10 years in Arizona. The services below cover each stage: keeping a heater healthy, fixing it when it breaks, and replacing it when its time is up.

Repair Services: Fixing What Failed

Repair is the right answer when the heater is relatively young and one component has failed. Heating elements burn out, thermostats drift, pressure relief valves start weeping, and gas pilot assemblies clog with dust. A contractor tests each part, replaces the failed one, and verifies the heater runs correctly before leaving.

Emergency repair exists for the failures that cannot wait: a tank leaking onto the garage floor, a complete loss of hot water, or a gas smell near the unit. These calls jump the queue because water damage and safety concerns do not wait for a convenient time.

Gas and electric heaters fail in different ways, and the network includes contractors experienced with both. Gas work centers on burners, pilots, thermocouples, and venting; electric work centers on elements, thermostats, and wiring. Knowing which type you have helps the contractor arrive with the right parts.

Installation and Replacement: Starting Fresh

Replacement becomes the practical choice when the tank itself is compromised. A tank leaking from the shell cannot be patched, and a unit past its expected life in Valley water will keep producing repair bills. Contractors assess the age and condition of the existing heater, then recommend a replacement sized to the household.

Installation covers both same-type swaps and upgrades. Many Tempe homeowners use a replacement as the moment to switch from tank to tankless, or from an aging electric tank to a gas unit where gas service exists. The contractor handles the physical install, the gas or electric connections, venting, drain pans, expansion tanks, and removal of the old unit.

Correct sizing matters more than most homeowners realize. An undersized tank runs out of hot water during back-to-back showers; an oversized one wastes energy. Contractors size by household members, bathrooms, and usage patterns rather than guessing from the old unit's label.

Tankless Water Heater Installation and Repair

Tankless units heat water on demand instead of storing it, which makes them a natural fit for Valley homes. They take up far less space than a 40 or 50 gallon tank, they never run out of hot water mid-shower, and they suit Arizona's mild winters, where incoming water temperatures are moderate year round.

They are not maintenance-free, though. Hard water leaves scale inside the heat exchanger, so tankless units in the Valley need periodic descaling to keep flow rates and efficiency up. Contractors perform descaling service, diagnose ignition and flow-sensor faults, and handle full installations including gas line sizing and venting.

A tankless conversion is a bigger project than a tank swap: gas units need adequate gas supply and proper venting, and electric models need substantial electrical capacity. A contractor evaluates the home first and tells you honestly whether a conversion is practical for your setup.

Maintenance: Protecting Your Investment

Flushing is the single most effective maintenance task for a tank heater in the Valley. Draining the tank once a year removes the sediment that hard water deposits, restoring heating efficiency and quieting the popping and rumbling that sediment causes. Contractors also inspect the anode rod during the visit and recommend replacement when it is depleted.

A maintenance visit usually includes a thermostat check, a pressure relief valve test, and a look at the venting and connections. The contractor leaves you with an honest assessment of the heater's condition and remaining life, which is far more useful than guessing.

Homeowners with tankless units need descaling on a regular schedule instead of tank flushing. Skipping it lets scale choke the heat exchanger, driving up energy use and eventually triggering error codes. Contractors set descaling intervals based on your water's hardness and your usage.

Choosing the Right Service for Your Situation

Start with the age of your heater. Under six years old with a specific failure, repair is usually the answer. Between six and ten years, it depends on the condition of the tank and the cost of the fix relative to a new unit. Past ten years in Valley water, replacement is often the smarter move even if a repair is technically possible.

Then consider the symptoms. No hot water at all points to a failed element, thermostat, or gas control. Lukewarm water or fast-depleting hot water suggests sediment buildup or a failing lower element. Water on the floor demands immediate attention, especially if it is coming from the tank shell rather than a fitting.

When in doubt, request service and let the contractor diagnose. A proper diagnosis costs less than a wrong guess, and the contractors in this network explain their findings before proposing any work. The service pages linked below go deeper into each option.

How do I know whether my water heater needs repair or replacement?

Age is the biggest clue. Heaters under six years old are usually good repair candidates, while units past eight to ten years in Valley water often make better replacement candidates. A contractor inspects the tank's condition and the failed part, then recommends the option that makes sense for your situation.

What does a water heater flushing service include?

The contractor drains the tank to remove sediment, inspects the anode rod, tests the thermostat and pressure relief valve, and checks venting and connections. You get an assessment of the heater's condition and an honest read on its remaining life.

Can I switch from a tank water heater to a tankless unit?

Often yes, but it is a bigger project than a straight tank swap. Gas tankless units need adequate gas supply and proper venting, and electric models need significant electrical capacity. A contractor evaluates your home first and tells you whether a conversion is practical.

What should I do if my water heater is leaking?

First, shut off the water supply to the heater and, for electric units, the power at the breaker. For gas units, turn the gas control to the off or pilot position if you can do so safely. Then request emergency service. A leak from the tank shell itself means the unit needs replacement.

How long does a water heater replacement take?

A straightforward tank swap is usually completed in a single visit. Tankless conversions and jobs with venting or gas line changes take longer. The contractor gives you a realistic timeline after seeing your setup.

Do contractors service both gas and electric water heaters?

Yes. The network includes contractors experienced with gas units, including burners, pilots, and venting, and with electric units, including elements, thermostats, and wiring. Mention your fuel type when requesting service so the contractor arrives prepared.
